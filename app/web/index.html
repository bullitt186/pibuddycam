    Image rotation

    Clockwise rotation of the sensor image, applied to every stream, snapshot and timelapse frame. Applying restarts the video streams briefly; the setting survives reboots and quality changes.

    90° and 270° use a slower rotation path: higher CPU load, possibly a reduced frame rate and higher latency, and portrait output (width and height swap).

    Wiring & printer setup

    1. Wiring

    The Hackerboard outputs are open-drain (they connect the pin to ground when active), so the Pi reads them directly with its internal pull-up. No level shifter and no extra parts are needed.

    • Hackerboard OUT0 → Pi header pin 11 (GPIO17): layer pulse
    • Hackerboard OUT1 → Pi header pin 13 (GPIO27): recording
    • Hackerboard GND → Pi header pin 9 (ground)

    Any of OUT0–OUT7 works; OUT0–OUT3 need no jumper. The G-code below assumes OUT0 (and OUT1 for recording).

    Never connect a printer voltage (24 V or 5 V) to the Pi. Do not add an external pull-up to anything other than 3.3 V; the Pi’s internal pull-up is sufficient.

    2. PrusaSlicer → Printer Settings → Custom G-code

    Start G-code (add at the end):

    M262 P0 B0

    With the recording pin, also add these lines. They force a clean start edge, so a print that was cancelled without its end G-code never leaves the pin stuck; the stale session is then closed and built and a fresh one opens:

    M262 P1 B0
M264 P1 B0
G4 P300
M264 P1 B1

    End G-code (add at the end): ends the recording and starts the automatic build.

    M264 P1 B0

    After layer change G-code:

    G1 X160 Y160 F{travel_speed*60}
G4 S0
M264 P0 B1
G4 P100
M264 P0 B0
G4 S8

    The dwell must be longer than the time from the pulse to the stored frame. No measurement yet: 8 s is a starting point. It is pre-filled from the measured latency plus a margin once a frame has been captured.

    The explicit 100 ms pulse is deliberate: back-to-back toggle commands can produce a pulse too short to be detected reliably.

    3. Reference

    The Prusa help article “GPIO module” describes the Hackerboard pins and the M262/M264 commands. The user guide of this project repeats the wiring and links to it.

    Wi-Fi & IP address

    If the new network does not come up within about a minute, the camera returns to the previous settings on its own. If even that fails it starts the setup hotspot.

    Hostname

    The console’s certificate is regenerated at the next boot to match a new name; reboot the camera to finish.

    Time zone

    Applies at once and again after every reboot. This is the camera’s own zone; the zone reported to Prusa Connect is detected separately.

    Time servers

    Blank means automatic: the server your router advertises, then the Debian pool. Set your own on networks that block outbound NTP.
